    Prediction markets and currency strategies

    Do Kalshi and Polymarket carry information that helps trade currencies, and where does that information sit?

    Romain Almeida, Piero Pelosi, Hino Wirachapong, Thomas Claudel. Sample {{sample_start}} to {{sample_end}}; in-sample 2024-2025, out-of-sample from {{oos_start}}. Every primary rule was logged before its backtest; post hoc additions are labelled. The page complements the 8 October 2026 presentation; one command rebuilds it from the replication package.

    Prediction markets trade the odds of events: a Fed cut, an election, a ceasefire. From {{pm_markets}} contracts we build one daily index per country and theme, and test the indices in two strategies: a G10 dollar strategy driven by US policy odds (Strategy A) and an emerging-market carry timed by a global geopolitical index (Strategy B).

    Three results

    Timing

    Prediction markets move with currencies, not before them.

    Same day: US monetary news and the dollar move together (t = {{t_mon_0}}). Next day: nothing (t = {{t_mon_1}}). Weekend test: nothing either (largest |t| = {{wk_max_t}} in its pre-registered specification). Section 5

    Content

    1. Economic framework

    Three course mechanisms set the sign of every trade

    No trading rule is estimated: the theory below sets each position's direction, so nothing can be overfitted. Pick a mechanism.

    What the theory says

    rx ≈ (i* − i) + Δs

    The excess return on a currency is its rate i* minus the dollar rate i, plus its appreciation Δs. Uncovered interest parity (UIP) says E[Δs] = i − i*, so carry should earn nothing.

    UIP holds→high-rate currency falls by the rate gap→carry = 0

    What the data say

    High-rate currencies fall by less than the rate gap. The literature reads the resulting carry premium as compensation for crash risk: carry loses in bursts during global risk-off episodes, when investors can least afford it.

    UIP fails→high-rate currency falls by less→carry earns a premium→paid for bearing crash risk

    Used in Strategy B. Rank six floating EM currencies by one-month forward premium (the rate gap); hold the top two against the bottom two. Can prediction markets see the crashes coming? Section 7

    Two kinds of news move the dollar in Strategy A. Monetary news is simple: a hawkish shift in expected Fed policy raises US rates and the dollar in every regime. Fiscal news depends on who controls the price level.

    Monetary dominanceFiscal dominance
    Who leadsThe central bank sets rates freely; the Treasury adjusts.The deficit is expected to be accommodated: the central bank will keep rates low or let inflation erode the debt.
    A bigger deficit meansMore bonds to sell, so higher yields; the currency is still a safe store of value.A risk premium on the country's assets: yields rise too, but as compensation for the risk of inflation or default.
    CapitalFlows in, attracted by the higher yields.Leaves, despite the higher yields.
    CurrencyRises.Falls.
    Tell-tale signYields and the currency move together.Yields up, currency down: the "sell everything" pattern.
    ExamplesThe United States most of the time.The United Kingdom in September 2022; the United States in April and May 2025.
    In Strategy A, higher odds of a fiscal expansion meanLong the dollar.Short the dollar.
    deficit odds ↑→yields ↑, capital in→dollar ↑monetary dominance
    deficit odds ↑→risk premium, capital out→dollar ↓fiscal dominance

    How the regime is detected. A negative rolling 60-day correlation between daily changes in the 10-year yield and in the currency signals fiscal dominance. The indicator flipped to the fiscal sign on {{regime_days_25}} days of 2025 ({{regime_share_25}}) and never in 2024 or 2026, so Strategy A effectively ran with the monetary-dominance sign. Section 6

    Exporters

    oil ↑→more dollars earned→BRL, MXN, COP ↑

    Higher prices for what a country sells improve its terms of trade: more export revenue, a stronger currency.

    Importers

    oil ↑→bigger energy bill→KRW ↓

    Higher prices for what a country buys worsen its terms of trade and weaken its currency.

    Why it matters here. Strategy B's carry book is long BRL, MXN and COP and short KRW and CLP, so it is long oil. In our window the oil exposure came from the short KRW and CLP legs and from COP; BRL and MXN moved little with Brent. Middle East escalation lifts oil, so the book gains from the very shocks the overlay was built to cut. Section 7

    Other course concepts used on this page
    IRR regime classification
    Strategy B's EM universe keeps only managed or freely floating currencies (Ilzetzki, Reinhart and Rogoff fine codes 12 and 13). Pegs and bands are excluded: their low volatility hides the real risk, a devaluation.
    CIP and forward pricing
    Carry comes from one-month forward and NDF points; the FX excess return is the spot change plus the forward-implied carry.
    Downside-risk CAPM
    Section 8 reports normal and downside betas (Lettau, Maggiori and Weber) to equities and to an EM carry factor.
    Efficient markets
    Section 5 finds that prediction-market moves do not lead the dollar at daily, weekend or hourly horizons, as efficiency predicts for liquid G10 FX.

    4. Signals

    How the signals are built, step by step

    One chain runs for every contract and cell. Each step shows its formula with real numbers from one of three examples: a Russia-Ukraine deadline contract, the US monetary cell around the December 2024 Fed meeting, and the geopolitics cell during the June 2025 Israel-Iran war.

    Scroll: the chart follows the steps on the right. Hover for values.

    1. A contract

    "{{walk_title}}" on {{walk_platform}}: the price in cents is the market's probability of a ceasefire before the {{walk_deadline}} deadline.

    2. Remove the deadline

    A "by date" contract drifts to zero as the deadline nears, even when nothing happens. The hazard rate, the chance per year given no event yet, keeps the news and drops the calendar.

    3. Standardise and sign

    Each day's change in λ is divided by the contract's usual move, signed so up means escalation (a ceasefire is {{walk_sign_word}}) and capped at ±{{winsor}}. On {{walk_peak_day}} the price jumped from {{walk_peak_px_before}} to {{walk_peak_px}} cents: z = {{walk_peak_z}}.

    4. Average into the cell

    The volume-weighted average z of eligible geopolitics contracts is the cell's daily change; its running sum is the global geopolitics index.

    5. Measure escalation

    Strategy B smooths the daily changes (EWMA, {{geo_halflife}}-day half-life) and z-scores the result on data available that day. At month-end, a z-score below {{esc_z1}} keeps full carry, {{esc_z1}} to {{esc_z2}} halves it, above {{esc_z2}} removes it.

    6. Scale the carry book

    A persistent daily move above {{shock_k}} standard deviations is a confirmed shock: exposure drops to {{shock_cut}}% (or the month-end weight if lower), then climbs back linearly to the month-end weight over {{rerisk_days}} days. Line: exposure applied to the carry book. Dots: confirmed shocks.

    7. The same chain for Strategy A

    The US monetary and fiscal cells take the same steps, then {{halflife}}-day smoothing and expanding scaling. The forecast y = −ZMON − (1 − 2R) ZFIS sets the sign of the dollar position: hawkish news, long dollar.

    The resulting theme indices

    Each index is the running sum of a cell's signed, standardised daily contract changes. Up means hawkish, escalation or fiscal expansion. Pick a cell; the bars below show how many contracts back it each day.

    Index level in standard deviations of contract changes; shaded area = out-of-sample. Drag the handles on the strip below the chart to zoom. Event markers (hover for the event) are illustrations only, never used by a trading rule.

    The other contract types: probabilities and bracket markets
    • Contracts without a deadline (for example "Will the Fed hike at the March meeting?") enter as their probability, with the same standardisation.
    • Bracket markets (Fed decision in basis points, inflation prints, number of cuts) become one expected value per meeting or print: the price-weighted average of bucket values, renormalised over the priced buckets. It then goes through the same change, volatility and cap steps.
    • Every contract must pass the section 2 eligibility rules each day. Robustness checks replace volume weights with equal weights.

    7. Strategy B, emerging markets

    EM carry timed by a geopolitical index

    The carry works: net Sharpe {{bc_net}} (interval {{bc_ci_lo}} to {{bc_ci_hi}}), {{bc_oos}} out of sample in 2026. The prediction-market overlay looks better at first ({{pm_net}}, drawdown {{pm_dd}} vs {{bc_dd}}), but its whole edge is one contract in one month.

    Mechanism 1 of 2: carry rule, universe and long history

    Mechanism 1 of 2: the carry pre-registered

    1. Sort on carry, monthly. Rank the six currencies by one-month forward or NDF premium (the interest gap).
    2. Long the top, short the bottom. Top tercile against bottom (2 against 2), inverse-volatility weights, {{vol_target}}% volatility target.
    3. Collect the premium. UIP fails: high-yielders fall by less than the rate gap.

    Universe. Floating currencies only (IRR codes 12 and 13). KRW closes before the {{snapshot_time}} London signal, so it trades with a one-day lag.

    Mechanism 2 of 2: the overlay pre-registered

    Each day, about a dozen liquid conflict contracts (Iran, Ukraine, Taiwan) form one global geopolitics index, scaled and signed so up means escalation. Two rules act on it.     10. Inference, limitations, replication

    How much these numbers can be trusted

    Pre-registration

    Every primary rule logged before its backtest; post hoc additions labelled.

    Out-of-sample

    Anything estimated uses 2024-2025 only; {{oos_start}} to {{sample_end}} is reported separately.

    Uncertainty

    Block-bootstrap {{boot_level}}% intervals ({{boot_block}}-day blocks, {{boot_draws}} draws). With under three years, a Sharpe below about 1.2 is not significant.

    Placebos

    Date shuffles, random contracts and reversed signs for every rule, including the a posteriori ones.

    Costs

    Half the forward bid-ask, tested from 0.5x to 3x.

    Report

    Every declared variant is reported, good and bad; read results with multiple testing in mind.

    What the data do not support

    • Next-day predictability of G10 FX from US policy odds: one-day predictive t-statistics are below 1 and the weekend test is null.
    • Country-relative G10 signals: non-US cells had almost no liquid contracts in 2024-2025. This is changing (UK fiscal at {{cov_uk_fis_26}} in 2026).
    • Carry protection from a geopolitical overlay: the window had no carry crash to insure, Middle East escalation helped a long-oil book, and the overlay's gain rests on one contract in one month.
    • The 20-day rule: its horizon was chosen after seeing the data and its 2026 Sharpe is mostly rebalancing-phase luck.
    • Fiscal sign conventions: shutdown contracts use a generic "higher fiscal risk" sign, election contracts cost A1 money in 2024-2025, and the fiscal-dominance indicator was active on {{regime_days_25}} days.
    • Costs rely on Bloomberg composite bid-ask quotes, probably wider than dealable spreads; A's daily turnover makes its net Sharpe very sensitive to this.
    • {{n_years}} years of data ({{n_days}} trading days): every confidence interval on this page is wide.
